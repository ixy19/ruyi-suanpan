【為什麼我們買得起百吋大電視，卻越來越不敢生小孩與生病？】

這是一張非常經典的經濟數據圖表（由學者 Mark J. Perry 根據美國勞工統計局 BLS 數據製作），記錄了從 2000 年到 2026 年這二十幾年來，各種生活開銷與薪水的漲跌幅度。看完只覺得 生活大不易

🔴 第一個宇宙：昂貴的「生存黑洞」

線條一路往右上角飆升的，全都是我們避無可避的生活剛需：

醫療費用（+291%）：高居榜首，生一次大病簡直像付房屋頭期款。

大學學費與教科書（+180%~200%）：翻了兩兩三倍，想翻身先背一屁股學學貸款。

托育與幼稚園（+162%）：養小孩成了極致的奢侈品。

房價與餐飲（+100%~115%）：基本吃住通通翻倍。

再看看那條夾在中間的紅線——平均時薪（+135%）。看似漲了不少，但只要跟醫療、教育和托育一比，瞬間就被殺得片甲不長。

🔵 第二個宇宙：便宜的「科技紅利」

另一半一路掉到地平線底下的，全是製造業與科技產品：

電視（-98%）：以前電視是客廳的昂貴大件，現在便宜到像買玩具。

玩具、軟體、通訊費（-44% ~ -73%）：通通大折價，買娛樂比以前輕鬆太多。

💡 這張圖告訴我們什麼？

這張圖完美的解釋了什麼叫「娛樂很廉價，生存很昂貴」。

現代人的生活現狀就是：我們可以用極低廉的成本，享受到人類歷史上最高級的視聽娛樂——買一台 85 吋的液晶電視、辦個便宜的電信吃到飽、玩著免費或廉價的遊戲，窩在房間裡快樂一整晚。

但是，當你抬頭面對真實人生——想要買一間房子、想要生個孩子送去托育、想要讓孩子上大學，或者只是老了需要看病照顧，那個壓力會一瞬間把你壓得喘不過氣。

科技進步帶來了便宜的物質與娛樂，卻無法解決高度依賴人力與本土服務的「剛性成本」。

最後的結果就是：大家越來越敢買新手機、換大電視，卻越來越不敢結婚、生小孩和生病。

這不是大家變懶了，也不是大家不努力了，而是這二十幾年來的經濟結構，把「好好生活」的門檻，築得太高了。

Why Can We Easily Buy a 85-Inch TV, But Fear Getting Sick or Having Kids?

I recently came across a classic economic chart created by Mark J. Perry, mapping out price changes in the US from 2000 to 2026 based on BLS data. Looking at it, one thought immediately comes to mind: Life is getting ridiculously tough.

If you look closely at the chart, modern life has essentially been split into two parallel universes:

🔴 Universe 1: The Escalating "Cost of Survival"

The red lines shooting up to the top right represent our unavoidable, essential needs:

Medical Services (+291%): Tops the list. Getting seriously ill feels like taking out a mortgage.

College Tuition & Textbooks (+180% to +200%): Up 2–3 times. Want social mobility? Start with a mountain of student debt.

Day Care & Childcare (+162%): Raising kids has become the ultimate luxury item.

Housing, Food & Beverages (+100% to +115%): Basic living costs have more than doubled.

And take a look at that middle line—Average Hourly Wages (+135%). It looks like a decent raise at first glance, but next to healthcare, education, and childcare, its purchasing power gets utterly wiped out.

🔵 Universe 2: The Shrinking "Tech Dividend"

On the flip side, the blue lines plummeting toward the floor are manufactured goods and tech products:

TVs (-98%): What used to be a major household investment is now cheaper than a toy.

Toys, Software, Cellphone Services (-44% to -73%): Heavy discounts across the board. Buying entertainment is easier than ever.

💡 What Does This Chart Really Tell Us?

It perfectly captures a modern paradox: "Entertainment is dirt cheap, but survival is sky-high."

The reality of modern living is that we can enjoy unprecedented levels of high-end entertainment at very low costs—buy an 85-inch smart TV, get an affordable unlimited data plan, play free-to-play games, and enjoy endless comfort inside our rooms.

Yet, the moment we look up and face real life—buying a home, sending a child to daycare, paying for college, or receiving care as we age—the financial pressure hits like a ton of bricks.

Technological progress brought us cheap gadgets and leisure, but it couldn't solve the rising costs of human-intensive, non-tradable local services.

The ultimate result? People feel totally fine buying new phones and bigger TVs, but increasingly hesitate to get married, have children, or fall ill.

It’s not that people have gotten lazy or stopped trying. The economic playbook of the last two decades has simply raised the barrier to a "standard life" higher than ever before.
